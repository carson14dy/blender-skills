# Review round 5: research note

**Nothing new changes a decision.** This round looked up two facts, because two flag rules lean on them. Both held. Everything else in round 5 came from measuring the 64 cached houses again, and is in the skill's `references/review-log.md`.

## 1. Asphalt shingles need 2 in 12 or more

- **Why it was checked.** Round 4 let a small plane with an unsettled pitch pass as a review item. Round 5 asked whether that could hide a plane that needs another material.
- **What the sources say.** The International Residential Code allows asphalt shingles only on slopes of 2 in 12 or steeper (R905.2.2). From 2 in 12 up to 4 in 12 it asks for a double layer of underlayment (R905.2.7). Several city handouts and a roofing guide repeat both limits.
- **What the skill does with it.** The small-plane rule now stays a site check when the city pitch and the lidar pitch fall on different sides of 2 in 12. The flag prints both pitches, so a reader also sees a pair that straddles 4 in 12.
- **Not checked.** Which code edition Sioux Falls has adopted, and its local amendments. The 2 in 12 limit is the same in every edition the sources quote. This is an inference for Sioux Falls, not a reading of the city ordinance.

## 2. One roll of ice barrier covers about 30 ft of eave

- **Why it was checked.** Round 4 made the `skewed-edge` flag an info item under 30 ft "because that little does not change an order".
- **What the sources say.** Ice barrier is sold in rolls of 36 in by about 66 ft, which is 200 sq ft, or about 186 sq ft after laps. The code asks for it from the eave edge to 24 in inside the wall line (R905.1.2), which on most houses means two courses, 6 ft up the roof.
- **What follows.** 30 ft of eave at two courses is 180 sq ft, about one roll. So the round 4 reason was wrong by up to one roll. The 30 ft line stays, because the flag still prints with the same words, and the docs now say what it can hide.
