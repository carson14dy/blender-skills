# Free city wireframes anchor accurate roof takeoffs

> **Superseded in part (2026-11-16).** This report was written before five review rounds checked it. Read `reports/Roof measurement research summary.md` first: it lists the corrections, such as measured lidar density, lidar setting the pitch, outline accuracy and the NAIP shift, and gives the current state of the `rooftec-roof-measure` skill.

The most useful free resource for a Sioux Falls roof studio is a dataset, not a program. The City of Sioux Falls publishes a **3D Building Wireframe** layer from June 2023: **95,901 closed 3D building solids made of planar roof faces, under CC BY 4.0**. A hands-on decode of four single-family homes showed three things. Roof faces were planar to within 0.24–0.39 ft. Plan areas matched the city footprints to the square foot. Face pitches clustered on standard framing values (5.6–6.1/12 on a roof that reads as 6/12). An independent check comes from the public-domain 2020 USGS QL2 lidar, whose measured vertical RMSE is about 4 cm. Process both with permissive libraries (PDAL, Open3D, three-mesh-bvh, proj4). Run the GPL roofer reconstructor as a separate program. On site, a taped baseline closes the last gap. Drone photogrammetry with OpenDroneMap or COLMAP gives centimetre-level relative geometry, but it is not strictly $0: the FAA Part 107 test costs about $175. Its scale is also only as good as the tape or ground control behind it. Two caveats apply to everything below. None of this free data has been checked against a field-measured Sioux Falls roof. And no free tool labels ridges, hips, valleys and eaves, so the studio has to write its own edge classifier. Google's Solar API and 3D Tiles, Nearmap, EagleView and the city's own 2026 Nearmap imagery are paid or view-only, so none can serve as a measurement source.

## Sioux Falls already publishes planar 3D roofs for 95,901 buildings

The wireframe layer (Community MapServer layer 66) is not a set of labelled roof lines, despite its name. **Each building is one `esriGeometryMultiPatch` solid**: vertical walls plus sloped or flat roof planes. Coordinates are in NAD83 BLM Zone 14N US survey feet (wkid 32164), and Z values are absolute elevations in feet. The layer description says only "3D Building wireframes layer, acquisition date June 2023." ([Layer JSON](https://gis.siouxfalls.gov/arcgis/rest/services/Data/Community/MapServer/66?f=pjson)). Every feature carries the same constant attributes, which are leftovers from a MicroStation-style CAD import: Entity "Cell", Layer "3101", RefName "BUILD". **No field marks ridge, hip, valley, eave or pitch.** The metadata names no vendor, capture method or accuracy ([layer metadata](https://gis.siouxfalls.gov/arcgis/rest/services/Data/Community/MapServer/66/metadata?f=xml)). A sibling contour layer was "generated by the Sanborn Map Company in April 2023" ([Contours layer](https://gis.siouxfalls.gov/arcgis/rest/services/Data/Community/MapServer/8?f=json)), which points to a 2023 Sanborn photogrammetric compilation, but nothing confirms it.

Decoding the compressed geometry for four south-side homes showed that the model holds real roof information. All figures in the table come from queries against the [wireframe query endpoint](https://gis.siouxfalls.gov/arcgis/rest/services/Data/Community/MapServer/66/query) and the [footprint query endpoint](https://gis.siouxfalls.gov/arcgis/rest/services/Data/Property/MapServer/5/query).

On house 235245, the main ridge runs about 53.5 ft horizontally and drops only 0.12 ft along its length. The largest face rises 9.75 ft over a 20.28 ft run, which is **5.77/12**. The sloped total is **3,269 sq ft (32.7 squares before waste)**, 1.11 times plan area, against a theoretical 1.118 for 6/12. The roof faces fit their planes to within 0.24–0.39 ft. On all four houses, the plan area of the roof faces equalled the footprint polygon's area exactly. That implies the city footprints are **roof outlines that include overhang**, not foundation lines. That is the outline a roofer wants, but it also means the wireframe cannot measure wall lines or overhang depth. These are inferences, not measurements. A spread of about ±0.5/12 across faces of a roof that is presumably uniform is a rough indicator of noise. The 3–3.5/12 reading on house 235246 sits below typical shingle minimums and deserves a field check before anyone trusts it.

The companion **Building Footprints** layer (Property MapServer layer 5) holds about 100,000 polygons. Consultants capture it on a 2–4 year cycle. Its fields include `GROUNDELEVATION`, `BUILDINGTOPELEVATION`, `NUMBERSTORIES` and `CAPTUREMETH` ([Footprints layer](https://gis.siouxfalls.gov/arcgis/rest/services/Data/Property/MapServer/5?f=json)). The city documents neither units nor datum. Ground elevations matched the USGS 1 m elevation service to within 0.9 ft, and three of four were within 0.2 ft ([USGS EPQS](https://epqs.nationalmap.gov/v1/json?x=-96.74243635172236&y=43.480905090381256&units=Feet&wkid=4326&includeDate=false)). The city's benchmarks carry a `NAVD88ELEV` field ([Benchmark layer](https://gis.siouxfalls.gov/arcgis/rest/services/Data/Community/MapServer/67?f=json)). Treat the elevations as NAVD88 feet. Top elevation tracks the wireframe ridge within about 1 ft, so it works as a free ridge-height check for any house. The wireframe's own flat base sits 1.9–8.3 ft below the footprint's ground elevation, so base-to-eave is not wall height. Address points add `YEARBUILT` and `CONSTRTYPE` ([Address layer](https://gis.siouxfalls.gov/arcgis/rest/services/Data/Property/MapServer/0)). A Building Permits layer can flag additions made after June 2023 ([Community MapServer](https://gis.siouxfalls.gov/arcgis/rest/services/Data/Community/MapServer)).

Getting the wireframe out takes some code. It is not on the city's DataWorks portal and has no ArcGIS Online item. `f=geojson` fails on this multipatch layer. Only Esri JSON works, with zlib-compressed `binaryPatches` that need a small decoder. Queries cap at 1,000 records but paginate ([query endpoint](https://gis.siouxfalls.gov/arcgis/rest/services/Data/Community/MapServer/66/query)). An envelope query over one footprint returns exactly one building, roughly 25–35 KB of base64, so per-job retrieval is trivial. The metadata licenses the data under CC BY 4.0 and adds that it is "intended for broad planning purposes only" and "not intended for use in mapping products for resale" ([ISO metadata](https://gis.siouxfalls.gov/arcgis/rest/services/Data/Community/MapServer/66/metadata?format=default&f=xml)). Internal estimating with "City of Sioux Falls" attribution reads as permitted. Selling the models as a map product does not. That is a reading of the licence text, not legal advice. Edge lengths take real cleanup: a crude topology pass inflated rake and hip totals through split edges and roof-tier steps. The lengths are in the geometry, but the studio must merge collinear segments and tell hips from valleys by convexity.

## QL2 lidar from 2020 checks pitch but cannot place eaves

Both counties are covered by **USGS 3DEP SD_EasternSD_3_2020**. It is QL2 under Lidar Base Specification 2.1, flown with a Leica ALS80 between April and December 2020 ([3DEP index](https://index.nationalmap.gov/arcgis/rest/services/3DEPElevationIndex/MapServer/24)). Its accuracy report gives **classified-point RMSEz of 3.6 cm and NVA of 7.1 cm** ([accuracy report](https://prd-tnm.s3.amazonaws.com/StagedProducts/Elevation/metadata/SD_EasternSD_2020_B20/vertical_accuracy/accuracy_report/USGS_SD_EasternSD_Block1_Project_Accuracy_Report.pdf)). The report is labelled "Block 1", and whether those figures apply to the Sioux Falls work unit is unconfirmed. The data is streamable with no account as a 42.65-billion-point Entwine dataset on AWS ([ept.json](https://s3-us-west-2.amazonaws.com/usgs-lidar-public/SD_EasternSD_3_2020/ept.json)), and it is public domain. One detail shapes the pipeline. **The point cloud has no building class (class 6).** Roofs sit in class 1 "Unclassified" alongside trees ([LPC QA report](https://prd-tnm.s3.amazonaws.com/StagedProducts/Elevation/metadata/SD_EasternSD_2020_B20/SD_EasternSD_3_2020/reports/USGS_SD_EasternSD_3_2020_FINAL_LPC_Report.txt)). The studio has to isolate roof points itself: crop to the city footprint, compute height above ground, and keep points more than about 2 m up.

QL2 means at least 2 points/m² and no more than 0.71 m spacing ([USGS Lidar Base Specification 2020 rev A](https://d9-wret.s3.us-west-2.amazonaws.com/assets/palladium/production/s3fs-public/atoms/files/Lidar-Base-Specification-2020-rev-A.pdf)). A 20 m² facet therefore holds roughly 40 points, and a 3 m² dormer cheek about six. The statistical slope error of a least-squares plane over a 4 m run with 40 points works out to roughly **0.8°**. That is an estimate, not a benchmark. Real errors are more likely systematic: points assigned to the wrong facet near ridges, footprint misalignment, and dormers. The published evidence is thin. Studies that use raster-slope GIS methods for rooftop solar report tilt RMSE of 6.8–7° ([Processes 2026](https://doi.org/10.3390/pr14101592); [Energies 2018](https://www.mdpi.com/1996-1073/11/12/3506)). These come from search summaries, and plane fitting should beat raster slope. A Salamanca study reports roof-area RMSE of **2.63% at 2 pts/m²**, against 5.25% at 0.5 pts/m² ([USAL repository](https://gredos.usal.es/bitstream/handle/10366/149568/Art2_Multi-scale.pdf?sequence=3&isAllowed=y)). Sánchez-Aparicio et al. found 1–2 pts/m² gave "a great improvement" in roof area over 0.5 pts/m², with diminishing returns at 14 pts/m² ([Remote Sensing 2020](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-4292/12/22/3726/htm)). **No independent study compares lidar plane-fit pitch with tape or inclinometer readings on U.S. homes**, so the studio has to measure that itself.

The lidar's weakness is edges. With 0.7 m spacing, eave and rake lines taken from lidar alone are uncertain by about ±0.35 m, which is 5–8% of plan area on a typical house. The sound division of labour follows from that. **The footprint or wireframe supplies the plan outline, lidar supplies pitch, and sloped area = plan area / cos(pitch).** Lidar is most valuable as an independent witness. If its plane-fit pitch on a large facet agrees with the wireframe face within about 1°, the job is probably clean. If they disagree, flag it for a site check. The lidar is also three years older than the wireframe, so a disagreement can mean a remodel.

The free reconstruction tool closest to the wireframe's output is **roofer** from the 3DBAG team (GPL-3.0, v1.0.0 released 2026-04-20). It takes LAS/LAZ plus a footprint and produces LoD2.2 piecewise-planar roofs as CityJSONSeq. It detects planes by region growing, intersects them into roof lines, and assigns faces by graph-cut optimisation ([roofer README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/3DBAG/roofer/HEAD/README.md); [algorithm docs](https://innovation.3dbag.nl/roofer/reconstruct_params.html)). It needs points classified as ground (2) and building (6), works best at "~10 pts/m²", and cannot model overhangs ([input requirements](https://innovation.3dbag.nl/roofer/data_requirements.html)). QL2 is four to five times sparser than that, so expect dormers and crickets to merge or vanish. The city footprints are roof outlines, which suits roofer's "roofprint" input. Install it from release binaries or the `3dgi/roofer` Docker image. **Do not `pip install roofer`**: that PyPI name belongs to an unrelated tile tool ([PyPI roofer](https://pypi.org/pypi/roofer/json)). 3DBAG's published per-building fields are a ready-made QA template for the studio's own outputs: model-to-points RMSE (`b3_rmse_lod22`), per-part slope and azimuth, and point density ([3DBAG attributes](https://docs.3dbag.nl/en/schema/attributes/)).

For imagery, **NAIP 2025 is 0.3 m and public domain**. It was flown on 2025-07-28 over Sioux Falls and is readable anonymously from Microsoft Planetary Computer ([Planetary Computer NAIP](https://planetarycomputer.microsoft.com/api/stac/v1/collections/naip)). It is leaf-on, so trees hide some eaves, but it is good enough to confirm the outline and spot changes since 2023. The city's newer **2026 6-inch Nearmap tiles are view-only**: "The imagery cannot be made available for download," and the tile cache stops at zoom level 20 ([2026 Imagery item](https://www.arcgis.com/home/item.html?id=028eceb03d874679a319960bf2605ca0)). A DataWorks link to a CC BY "Open Data/Imagery" OneDrive folder returned HTTP 401 to scripted access, so its contents are unknown ([Imagery link item](https://www.arcgis.com/home/item.html?id=fc1859a2592145418ab51025d55ab872)). For addresses outside city limits, use Microsoft's footprints (CDLA-Permissive-2.0, with 2026 U.S. updates and heights) ([GitHub](https://github.com/microsoft/GlobalMLBuildingFootprints)). Overture buildings are ODbL, whose share-alike terms apply to derived databases ([Overture guide](https://docs.overturemaps.org/guides/buildings/)).

Several "free" sources should stay out of the measurement chain. They need billing accounts or carry terms that forbid extracting and storing measurements. The research notes flag Google's Solar API and Photorealistic 3D Tiles, Cesium ion's non-commercial free tier, Esri's imagery basemap, and paid parcel resellers. Those terms were not re-checked in November 2026, so confirm them before relying on that reading. The city's own [Nearmap subscription](https://cf.nmws.nearmap.com/customer-stories/detailed-aerial-maps-in-sioux-falls) does not extend to contractors.

## A taped baseline matters more than the drone

Some simple geometry shows where effort pays. Area scales with the square of linear scale. **A 1% area target therefore allows only about 0.5% linear error, which is 5 cm over a 10 m eave.** At 6/12, a 1° pitch error shifts area by about 0.9%. A full x/12 step is 3.7–4°, and on a 4 m run that is 0.33 m of rise. Centimetre-level vertical noise meets the pitch target easily. Scale is the hard part, and it is the one thing images cannot recover on their own.

The drone evidence supports this. WebODM's documentation puts relative accuracy at **1–3 times the ground sample distance (GSD)**, the ground width of one image pixel. Drone GPS alone is off by 2–6 m horizontally ([WebODM GCP docs](https://docs.webodm.org/ground-control-points/)). Scale from GPS alone varies a lot between flights. In a UK accident-investigation test, GPS-scaled distances came out 0.3–0.4% short, and **one taped scale bar cut the error to 0–1 cm** ([ICAO/AAIB](https://www.icao.int/sites/default/files/airnavigation/AIG/6-Hawkins-Using-a-drone-and-photogrammetry-software-V3.pdf)). One user's orthomosaic made without ground control measured a football field 7% short ([ODM community](https://community.opendronemap.org/t/poor-relative-accuracy-of-orthomosaic-without-ground-control/2994)). The best-known roof study, by Haag Engineering, found GPS-scaled drone areas within an average 0.6% of hand measurement on 13 roofs, but the worst roof was off by 2.7%. That study used a proprietary Kespry/Pointivo pipeline, not free software ([RT3 Think Tank](https://rt3thinktank.com/drone-photogrammetry-test-are-automated-3d-roof-measurements-accurate-enough/)). A FIG 2026 test of a consumer Mavic Air 2 with WebODM measured 3.38 m planimetric RMSE with no ground control and 0.085 m with four control points ([FIG 2026](https://fig.net/resources/proceedings/fig_proceedings/fig2026/papers/ts08d/TS08D_seboo_ramgoolam_et_al_14135_abs.pdf)). Rolling shutter matters on Mavic-class cameras, which expose the frame line by line. One comparison found 16.5 cm RMSE in WebODM against 2.46 cm in a commercial processor that corrects for it ([ODM community](https://community.opendronemap.org/t/ground-control-point-application-comparison/10481)). Fly slowly or hover for each shot.

The $0 software is mature. **OpenDroneMap/WebODM** (AGPL-3.0) outputs a georeferenced LAZ point cloud, a textured OBJ, a DSM and an orthophoto, and runs CPU-only in Docker ([ODM outputs](https://docs.opendronemap.org/outputs/)). It has no scale-bar constraint, so scale comes from ground control points or from rescaling afterwards ([ODM community](https://community.opendronemap.org/t/increase-relative-accuracy/21617)). **Find-GCP**, now an ODM project, detects printed ArUco markers and writes `gcp_list.txt` automatically ([Find-GCP](https://opendronemap.org/findgcp/)). Markers placed on the ground and taped against each other in local coordinates replace RTK GNSS. **COLMAP 4.0** (BSD, March 2026) folds in the GLOMAP global mapper and can align a model to custom, non-GPS coordinates ([COLMAP 4.0.0](https://github.com/colmap/colmap/releases/tag/4.0.0); [PR #1371](https://github.com/colmap/colmap/pull/1371)). Its dense stereo step needs a CUDA GPU ([issue #192](https://github.com/colmap/colmap/issues/192)). A recipe consistent with ODM and vendor guidance follows ([ODM flying tips](https://docs.opendronemap.org/flying/)). Fly a nadir grid at roof height plus 15–25 m, which gives about 1 cm GSD on a Mini 4 Pro in 12 MP mode. Add a 45° oblique orbit at 75–80% overlap and fly at 2–3 m/s. Then rescale using a taped eave or gutter run.

Flying is the one non-zero cost in the stack. **Commercial roof flights require a Part 107 certificate**: about $175 for the test, $5 per drone for registration, and free recurrent training ([FAA commercial operators](https://www.faa.gov/uas/commercial_operators); [FAA registration](https://www.faa.gov/uas/getting_started/register_drone)). DJI Fly's built-in waypoints are free. Dronelink's hobbyist plans exclude commercial use, and Map Pilot Pro needs a subscription ([Dronelink pricing](https://app.dronelink.com/pricing/hobbyist); [Map Pilot Pro](https://www.mapsmadeeasy.com/mappilotpro)).

The other capture methods each fill a narrow role.

| Method | Measured accuracy | Out-of-pocket cost | Commercially clean free software |
|---|---|---|---|
| Drone + ODM/COLMAP, taped scale or ArUco GCPs | 1–3× GSD relative; scale error 0–1 cm after one scale bar | Part 107 (~$175) and printed targets | COLMAP (BSD); ODM/WebODM (AGPL, internal use) |
| iPhone/iPad LiDAR from ladder or roof | 1–2 cm on objects; 3.7–8.5 cm RMS on a 46.5 m façade ([Hamdan 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s41651-026-00288-x)) | $0 on an owned Pro device | 3d Scanner App, SiteScape, KIRI Engine Basic |
| Square-on gable photo or deck inclinometer | No published accuracy | $0 | Exact Protractor, free inclinometer apps |
| Monocular depth / segmentation | Not validated for roofs | $0 | Depth Anything V2 Small (Apache-2.0), MoGe (MIT), SAM 2 (Apache-2.0) |

Phone LiDAR's range is only about 5 m ([How-To Geek](https://www.howtogeek.com/695823/what-is-lidar-and-how-will-it-work-on-the-iphone/)), so it cannot capture a whole roof from the ground. From a ladder it can verify one eave length or one plane's pitch. The app terms matter as much as the sensor. Polycam Free exports only glTF and allows commercial use of models with attribution ([Polycam ToS](https://poly.cam/legal/terms-of-service)). KIRI Engine Basic exports OBJ, PLY and GLB without limits on iOS, Android and web ([KIRI pricing](https://www.kiriengine.app/pricing)). **Scaniverse Classic forbids "commercial use/financial gain of any kind"** ([Niantic](https://community.nianticspatial.com/t/are-there-any-plans-for-this-to-be-available-in-a-license-for-students/5747)). For AI depth and splats, the trap is licensing, not capability. Metric3D weights need an emailed commercial request. UniDepth and MASt3R are non-commercial, and Apple Depth Pro is research-only. The original 2DGS, PGSR, SuGaR and GOF splat-to-mesh repos all prohibit commercial use ([PGSR LICENSE](https://github.com/zju3dv/PGSR/blob/c07d2de0b779471c70be88a1cf9bf7604679434d/LICENSE.md); [SuGaR LICENSE](https://github.com/Anttwo/SuGaR/blob/main/LICENSE.md)). Meta's separately gated **VGGT-1B-Commercial** checkpoint is the exception among feed-forward 3D models, which reconstruct a scene in a single network pass ([HF VGGT-1B-Commercial](https://huggingface.co/facebook/VGGT-1B-Commercial)). Splats are fine for the customer-facing Three.js visual. Measurements should come from the photogrammetric cloud.

## Permissive libraries carry the studio; GPL tools stay behind a file boundary

A clean licensing pattern runs through the toolset. The libraries that do the measuring maths are permissive. The best reconstruction programs are GPL or AGPL, and those are safe to run in-house as separate processes. GPL obligations apply when software is distributed. AGPL adds obligations when modified software is offered to outside users over a network. Output data such as CityJSON or roof JSON is not covered ([CGAL license page](https://www.cgal.org/license.html)). The practical rule: import permissive libraries anywhere, and call GPL tools through a CLI or file exchange. Never expose AGPL tools such as WebODM to customers as a web portal.

| Embed anywhere (permissive) | Run as a separate in-house program (GPL/AGPL) | Avoid for commercial work |
|---|---|---|
| PDAL and python-pdal (BSD), laspy (BSD-2), Open3D (MIT), pyRANSAC-3D (Apache-2.0), COLMAP (BSD), SAM 2 (Apache-2.0), three, three-mesh-bvh, proj4, turf, copc.js (MIT), laz-perf (Apache-2.0), earcut (ISC), straight-skeleton **1.x** (MIT) | roofer, City3D, PolyFit, CloudCompare, CGAL algorithm packages, bpypolyskel, QGIS, BlenderGIS, Point Cloud I/O, CAD Sketcher (GPL); ODM/WebODM, OpenMVS, geoflow building reconstruction (AGPL) | 2DGS, PGSR, SuGaR, GOF, MASt3R/DUSt3R, UniDepth, Depth Pro, Scaniverse Classic; repos with no license (point2building, point2wss) |

Sources: [PDAL](https://github.com/PDAL/PDAL), [Open3D LICENSE](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/isl-org/Open3D/HEAD/LICENSE), [pyRANSAC-3D](https://github.com/leomariga/pyRANSAC-3D), [CGAL packages](https://doc.cgal.org/latest/Manual/packages.html), [CloudCompare license](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/CloudCompare/CloudCompare/HEAD/license.txt), [openMVS](https://github.com/cdcseacave/openMVS/).

Two traps stand out. The npm **straight-skeleton** package is tagged MIT for every version. Since 2.0, however, it has wrapped **CGAL's GPL straight-skeleton code compiled to WebAssembly**, and only the 1.x line is pure TypeScript ([README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/StrandedKitty/straight-skeleton/HEAD/README.md); [npm](https://registry.npmjs.org/straight-skeleton)). Pin `^1.1.0` if the Three.js studio is ever shipped to customers. The second trap is City3D, which bundles the SCIP solver. Older SCIP versions carried a non-commercial academic licence, so check the bundled version before any commercial use. That is background knowledge from the notes, not verified. A straight skeleton turns a footprint into hip and ridge topology, assuming every eave has the same pitch. **bpypolyskel** (GPL-3.0) is the Blender-ready option, tested on all 320,000 hipped roofs in OpenStreetMap with a 99.99% success rate ([bpypolyskel README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/prochitecture/bpypolyskel/HEAD/README.md)). It is a useful fallback where no wireframe exists. Fit lidar planes per skeleton facet, and rebuild with roofer when the facet pitches disagree.

On the Blender side, the free, maintained set is small but enough. **Point Cloud I/O** (GPL-3.0, on extensions.blender.org) imports LAS/LAZ, E57 and PLY into Blender's native point cloud object, keeping classification as an attribute. Version 0.6.0 requires Blender 5.1+ ([extension page](https://extensions.blender.org/add-ons/point-cloud-io/)). MeasureIt 1.8.4 is fixed for Blender 5.0 ([MeasureIt versions](https://extensions.blender.org/add-ons/measureit/versions/)). CAD Sketcher adds constraint sketching ([GitHub](https://github.com/hlorus/CAD_Sketcher)), and the MIT Photogrammetry Importer brings in COLMAP cameras and points ([GitHub](https://github.com/SBCV/Blender-Addon-Photogrammetry-Importer)). BlenderGIS and blosm offer only roughly 30 m terrain, which is context, not roof geometry ([BlenderGIS](https://github.com/domlysz/BlenderGIS)). Point Cloud Visualizer ($85–$150) and Roof Generator are paid ([Superhive PCV](https://superhivemarket.com/products/pcv)). No free add-on fits roof planes to lidar automatically.

In Three.js, accuracy comes from two habits more than from any renderer. The first is **three-mesh-bvh**, which raycasts against a spatial index so clicks snap precisely to vertices and edges ([GitHub](https://github.com/gkjohnson/three-mesh-bvh)). The second is **proj4**, which projects everything to a metric CRS before computing lengths ([proj4js](https://github.com/proj4js/proj4js)). Subtract a local origin everywhere. The wireframe's state-plane coordinates run into the millions of feet, and roofer's docs warn about float precision for the same reason ([roofer Python API](https://innovation.3dbag.nl/roofer/api_py.html)). For a single lot, clip the lidar server-side with PDAL and load it as a plain `THREE.Points` array. copc.js and laz-perf are only needed to browse whole tiles ([copc.js](https://github.com/connormanning/copc.js)). opengeos' MIT `maplibre-gl-usgs-lidar` can export a PDAL pipeline that clips 3DEP to a drawn area, which makes a good reference for search logic ([README](https://github.com/opengeos/maplibre-gl-usgs-lidar)).

The most important code the studio will write is the **edge classifier**, and one module serves every source. The wireframe, roofer's CityJSON and planes fitted to drone data all reduce to planar faces with shared edges. Classify each edge as follows. A horizontal convex edge between two roof faces is a ridge. A sloped convex edge is a hip, and a concave one is a valley. A horizontal boundary edge where a roof face meets a wall is an eave, and a sloped one is a rake. Then merge collinear segments and drop wall steps between roof tiers. Roofer's docs confirm the face topology exists, and none of the tools emits these labels ([roofer algorithm](https://innovation.3dbag.nl/roofer/reconstruct_params.html)).

## No roof skill exists, so agents need discipline rather than plugins

No agent skill, MCP server or plugin measures roofs. Searches of skills.sh, GitHub and Anthropic's official marketplace found none, and the marketplace's only geo plugins are vendor platforms that require accounts ([marketplace.json](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/anthropics/claude-plugins-official/HEAD/.claude-plugin/marketplace.json)). The worthwhile skills supply method rather than roof maths. **muend/geoai-skills** (MIT) has a `point-cloud-lidar` skill covering PDAL pipelines, "vertical datum agreement" and "the vertical-accuracy budget". It warns against "trusting vendor classification blindly" and "ignoring point density when choosing output resolution" ([SKILL.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/muend/geoai-skills/HEAD/skills/point-cloud-lidar/SKILL.md)). Those are exactly the failure modes of the 3DEP no-building-class problem. **kylefritz/3d-model-skill** (MIT) shows a provenance pattern in which "every dimension says where it came from (tape, scan, spec sheet, estimate)" ([README](https://github.com/kylefritz/3d-model-skill)). That fits a takeoff that blends wireframe, lidar and tape. opengeos/geoai-skills can pull NAIP by bounding box and year ([README](https://github.com/opengeos/geoai-skills)), and a 19-skill PyQGIS pack covers QGIS scripting ([README](https://github.com/Impertio-Studio/QGIS-Claude-Skill-Package)). Most of these repos are under a year old with fewer than 100 stars. Vendor a pinned copy rather than tracking them live.

The MCP landscape repeats the pattern. **There is no PDAL MCP**, and the point-cloud MCPs found have 0–5 stars, some with no licence ([GitHub search](https://github.com/search?q=pdal+mcp&type=repositories)). An agent should call PDAL and Open3D directly from Python. sparkgeo's curated list is the best map of the field and labels which servers are open, commercial or stale ([sparkgeo/geo-mcp-servers](https://github.com/sparkgeo/geo-mcp-servers)). Useful entries include gdal-mcp (MIT), QgisRemoteMCP (MIT, the only QGIS bridge built for headless Docker), PlaceRoot (MIT, Overture buildings with no key), and ahujasid's Blender server, now `mcp-for-blender` (MIT). The Blender server collects "a minimal anonymous usage record" by default, and an environment variable disables it ([README](https://github.com/ahujasid/mcp-for-blender)). The pipeworx USGS National Map MCP caps anonymous use at 50 calls a day and routes data through a third-party gateway ([README](https://github.com/pipeworx-io/mcp-usgs-national-map)), so query the TNM API or Planetary Computer STAC directly. For headless GIS, QGIS has had native PDAL-based point-cloud processing since 3.32 ([QGIS 3.32 changelog](https://changelog.qgis.org/en/qgis/version/3.32/)), and `qgis_process` runs any Processing algorithm without the desktop ([QGIS docs](https://docs.qgis.org/3.44/en/docs/user_manual/processing/standalone.html)). Plugins installed in a container are not loaded automatically ([qgis/QGIS#41218](https://github.com/qgis/QGIS/issues/41218)).

## Conclusion

In Sioux Falls, the job is mostly verifying existing data, not rebuilding roofs from scratch. A city-compiled 2023 roof model probably beats anything open-source tools can reconstruct from 2 pts/m² lidar. The free toolchain's best use is checking that model and catching where reality has diverged from it. The single highest-value item to build is therefore a project-specific skill, not a third-party add-on. It would fetch and decode one wireframe, classify its edges, cross-check face pitches against 3DEP plane fits and footprint top elevation, flag permits issued after June 2023, and tag every dimension with its source. Everything it needs is permissive or public domain, except roofer, which stays behind a CLI boundary.

The missing piece is calibration evidence, and nobody has published it. EagleView's 98%-range accuracy figures come from a study EagleView commissioned that did not disclose its sample size ([The Roofing Brief](https://theroofingbrief.com/2026-aerial-roof-measurement-software-report/)). No independent study validates free lidar or free photogrammetry pitch on residential roofs. Taping and inclinometer-checking 5–10 Sioux Falls roofs the business is already working on would turn a plausible ±0.5/12 into a measured error band, and would show whether the city's 3/12 readings are real. That small dataset costs nothing but site time. It would also let the studio quote its own accuracy with more evidence behind it than the paid report vendors publish.
